The build timeline

35 commits5 phasesb4ae659 → dcee8c6 (v1)

The whole app is 35 commits over about thirteen hours. Read in order, the log tells a story that no finished codebase can: where the build flowed, and where it doubled back. Below it's grouped into five phases. The reversal and churn tags mark the teaching moments — the spots where the design changed its mind.

The reversalCommit e7f2843 colored work cards across the whole UI; the very next commit 47464b8 pulled that back to the schedule board only. Coloring everything turned the UI into confetti. This is normal. You cannot always see that an idea is too much until it's on the screen. The fix was cheap because the change was small and isolated — a 17/19-line diff.
The churnNavigation took three commits (641fd78 → 9c9993f → 162a4e8) to find its final shape: chips → section menu → simplified. UI navigation is genuinely hard to get right on the first try. Small commits made the wandering safe.

The good arcPagination is the model to copy: doc → server → client, each layer landed and verified before the next. Contrast with the Phase-3 big bang. Same engineer, same day — the difference is sequencing. (Full autopsy in S7.)

The shape of the work

Read the commit sizes and a pattern appears:

  • Two giant commits (the scaffold, the mega-refactor) carry most of the risk.
  • A long tail of tiny commits (nit, 1-line tweaks, label fixes) is where taste gets dialed in.
  • The best-sequenced feature (pagination) came last — after the engineer had felt enough pain to slow down and plan.

That arc — sprint, sprawl, then plan — is exactly what we'll straighten out in the rebuild.
